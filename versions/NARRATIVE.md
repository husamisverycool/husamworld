# Husam's story, for a VC and startup audience

Facts come from `CONTENT.md`. This file decides **which** facts lead, **in what order**, and **how he sounds**.

## Who is reading
Seed investors, founders, operators, and early employees he may recruit. They open the site after an intro, a tweet, or a demo day, and give it about 40 seconds. They're asking three things:
1. **What is he building?** (Stealth, so we answer with confidence, not detail.)
2. **Why him?** (Founder-market fit and a track record of getting things done.)
3. **Is he exceptional, and does he have taste?** (Outlier signals, shown with restraint. The site itself is proof of taste.)

They are allergic to résumé padding, "passionate", honor lists that run for pages, and anything that looks templated. They respond to specifics, numbers, speed, and a clear point of view.

## What to leave out or play down
- **Partisan roles** (High School Democrats, McCarthy intern) are fine in a full record but never in the headline. Keep the tone nonpartisan.
- **Long lists of small awards.** At most one "full record" section, and only if the design has a natural place for it.
- **Stealth rule (strict):** don't name the company, product, market, customers, pilots, pricing, raise, or timeline, and don't say he is leaving school. The only allowed lines are "Co-founder & CEO of a stealth startup, backed by DoorDash's early team" and "Spent summer 2026 building it full-time in San Francisco." A wink about stealth is good ("We'd tell you, but then it wouldn't be stealth").

## Voice
First person or crisp third person, and pick one per design. Short sentences. Numbers over adjectives. Confident, dry, a little funny. No invented opinions, beliefs, quotes, or testimonials: where a design needs his views, use a clearly marked placeholder like `[Husam: one sentence on why you're building this]`. Never write "passionate", "journey", "leveraging", "impact-driven", "changemaker", "serial entrepreneur", or "visionary".
