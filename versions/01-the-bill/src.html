Legislative history. Fresno and Clovis, 2015–2025. Cambridge, 2025–. English and Arabic.

SEC. 4. Definitions.

In this Act:

  1. (1) Husam.—The term “Husam” means Husam Sokar, a sophomore at Harvard College concentrating in Applied Math in Government and Economics.

  2. (2) Fresno.—The term “Fresno” includes Clovis, and means the part of California’s Central Valley that Husam called home from 2015 to 2025.

  3. (3) Bill.—The term “bill” means—

    1. (A) AB 1766, AB 1947, SB 1161, AB 2549, and SB 1038 of the California Legislature, as described in section 101; and

    2. (B) the billing Husam handled growing up, as an admin assistant in billing and back-office operations at his father’s law firm.

  4. (4) Working languages.—The term “working languages” means English and Arabic, translated professionally in either direction.

  5. (5) Weekend.—The term “weekend”, for the years 2015 through 2021, means most Sundays at the Central California Food Bank and, beginning in 2018, most Saturdays at Masjid Fresno Saturday School.

  6. (6) Method.—The term “method” means learning how an institution actually runs, and then doing the work inside it.

  7. (7) Stealth startup.—The term “stealth startup” has the meaning given in section 401, which is brief on purpose.

TITLE I—PUBLIC SERVICE AND ADVOCACY

Legislative history. Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR), Sacramento. Lead Advocate, May 2021–May 2023.

SEC. 101. California legislation.

(a) In General.—As Lead Advocate for CAIR in Sacramento, Husam was the lead community advocate for AB 1766 (California IDs for All), working with the office of Assemblymember Jim Patterson, through Legislative Director Ian Coolbear, and with the office of Senator Shannon Grove.

(b) Additional Measures.—Husam also advocated for—

  1. (1) AB 1947, Freedom from Hate Crimes;

  2. (2) SB 1161, Transit Safety;

  3. (3) AB 2549, Ending Street Harassment; and

  4. (4) SB 1038, limits on biometric surveillance by law enforcement.

(c) Count.—Five California bills, worked end to end.

Legislative history. U.S. House of Representatives, Clovis district office. June–December 2022.

SEC. 102. Service in the House of Representatives.

Husam served as a Congressional Intern in the Office of Kevin McCarthy in Clovis, California.

Legislative history. Board member from Sep 2021; also Director of Outreach, Director of Relations, and Director of External Relations. VP & Executive Director, May 2022–Dec 2025. Amdt. to subsec. (a): board member → Vice President and Executive Director.

SEC. 103. Muslim Community Association of Fresno.

(a) Office.—Husam served as a member of the board Vice President and Executive Director of the Muslim Community Association of Fresno, a youth-driven nonprofit.

(b) Record.—During Husam’s service, the Association—

  1. (1) held more than 150 community events;

  2. (2) raised more than $240,000 for Syrian and Afghan refugees and for victims of the floods in Pakistan;

  3. (3) secured a $500,000 pledge for Fresno’s first transitional home for Muslim women, addressing—

    1. (A) homelessness; and

    2. (B) domestic abuse; and

  4. (4) ran clothing and food drives and mentoring groups at local mosques and Islamic schools.

Legislative history. Volunteer, then Outreach Director. Executive Director, April–December 2022. Amdt.: volunteer → Executive Director.

SEC. 104. LegislativeLift.

Husam served as a volunteer Executive Director of LegislativeLift, a global Muslim civil-rights nonprofit of more than 11,000 members and more than 80 volunteers, with events in Egypt, India, and the United States, scholar programs, and work on legislative literacy.

Legislative history. NSDA, May 2024–May 2025. Diversify Our Narrative, Jun 2024–Jan 2025. CA High School Democrats, Jul 2024–Feb 2025. Voters of Tomorrow, Sep–Oct 2024. bayloop, Oct 2023–Dec 2024.

SEC. 105. National organizing.

(a) Speech and Debate.—Husam served on the Student Leadership Council of the National Speech & Debate Association, 1 of 23 students representing more than 140,000 members to the national board.

(b) Curriculum Equity.—As Executive Director of Operations of Diversify Our Narrative, Husam was 1 of 5 executives leading a national 501(c)(3) of more than 5,000 student organizers and more than 800 district chapters, featured in TIME, Bloomberg, Vox, ABC Nightline, and The Washington Post.

(c) Elections.—Husam was—

  1. (1) Managing Director of California High School Democrats, 1 of 9 directors managing all regions; and

  2. (2) an Election Fellow with Voters of Tomorrow, 1 of 50 paid fellows, phone banking and canvassing.

(d) Housing.—Husam founded bayloop, a Fresno think tank against restrictive zoning, which organized 7 youth phone banks across the Central Valley and did legislative research with the National Low-Income Housing Coalition.
