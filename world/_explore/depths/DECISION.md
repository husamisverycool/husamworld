# The Pier: which direction

Three working prototypes, all built only on the Pier's 21 honors (16 with a pool, 5 without).
Screenshots at 1440 and 390: `world/_shots/depths-r2/{a,b,c}-{d,p}-*.png` (contact sheets `*-sheet.png`).

- **a.html, the dive, elevated** (neal.fun/deep-sea). The round-1 engine rebuilt on the Pier's facts only:
  debate, Mock Trial, grants, journalism/civics programs, Ballotpedia, Jetson, photo, contact, stealth line
  and the sound toggle all gone. New deepest point: NSDA Student of the Year, 1 in 1,628, resting on the sand.
- **b.html, Forms of Rarity** (Haeckel's *Kunstformen der Natur* + museum labels). Six plates, each a
  rule-framed sheet of procedurally drawn creatures (class from the honor's kind: scholarships are medusae,
  leadership programs radiolaria, national recognitions diatoms), drawn more intricately the rarer they are,
  with a numbered "Erklärung" legend beside each plate.
- **c.html, The Size of the Pool** (neal.fun "The Size of Space"). One honor per step, arrow keys or swipe;
  the camera zooms out as each crowd grows, one lit point among 4, 17, 64 … 1,628 others in a sunflower spiral.

## Scores (1 to 5)

| | a. Dive | b. Plates | c. Pool zoom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fidelity to its inspiration | 5: the deep-sea skeleton, shader, counter, pacing | 4: plate anatomy is right; drawing is procedural, not lithographic | 4: title slide, step mechanic and bottom label match; no 3D |
| Ambition and memorability | 4: already strong; the ending was the weak part | 3: handsome, but it's a book you page through | 4: the zoom-out to 1,628 is a real "oh" |
| Fits HIS content | 5: rarity *is* depth, linear and exact | 3: grouping into plates is arbitrary; rarity shows only as ornament | 4: "1 of N" is literal; but the pool dots are rounded, and 5 honors have no pool to show |

| Never feels bad | 4: the empty stretches are the point, with exits always visible | 4: calm, no dead ends | 2: only one honor visible at a time; content hides behind interaction; nothing to scroll |

## The call: (a), elevated with the best of (b) and (c)

The current page wins, honestly: depth is the only metaphor where the numbers do the work, and every device
it uses is one the Pier owns. What it lacked was an ending of its own and a way to *see* the pool. So:

1. **From (c), the pool made visible, once, where it counts.** In the abyss, before the deepest point, the marine
   snow gathers into a sunflower spiral of 1,628 faint points; one of them is lit, and it turns out to be the
   anglerfish's lure. It's the Size-of-Space zoom, done as depth instead of as slides, and it only appears at the
   single honor that can carry it.
2. **From (b), the collection as a plate.** On the sand, the dive ends with a Haeckel-style plate: running head,
   rule frame, all 21 honors as numbered figures around a central axis (rarest at the centre, the five without a
   pool on the rim), a "Latin. — English." caption and a numbered legend. Drawn in the dive's own flat art and
   Neal's navy, Oswald and Heebo, not Haeckel's cream and serif. Every specimen on the way down carries its
   catalogue number, so the plate indexes the dive. This is the new ending, and it is only the Pier's.
3. **From Josh Worth's light-speed button, "let go".** A control that sinks you at a steady pace until you touch
   anything. The long scroll becomes something you can watch.
4. **From Spend, the tally.** The plate closes with the one sum the page can honestly make: fifteen separate pools
   (Quest for Excellence drew from the College Prep Scholars, so it isn't counted twice) add up to 438,400+
   people, and he was picked out of every one.

Rejected: (b)'s serif typography and facing-page spread (banned-look risk, Bridge device), and (c)'s one-at-a-time
slides (hides content, loses the depth).
