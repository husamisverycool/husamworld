## The deduction

No single direction wins outright, and each one fixes another's weakness:
- B is the memorable thing, but alone it hides facts behind play and ends in a dead end.
- C gives the page a spine and makes B honest: congress.gov already puts video of proceedings inside the record, so a cartoon player *inside* the overview box is at home there, and the **tracker chevrons become the scene buttons**.
- A is where the facts live, fully readable at rest, and it owns the Capitol's devices (line numbers, margin annotations, a signature pad and a rubber stamp), which give B its ending: the last stop on the tracker is **"Your signature"**, and signing the bill at the foot of the text stamps it ENROLLED and makes the cartoon bill jump.

**Chosen: C as the frame, B as the centrepiece, A as the text.** A congress.gov/leginfo record for H.R. 2026, whose overview box holds an eight-scene cartoon (click a chevron to watch that step), followed by the full typeset bill with line numbers, sidenotes and gwern-style popups on every bill number and organisation, a dated All Actions table with leginfo's two-lane rail, and the signature pad.

## What changes from round 1
- The giant `HUSAM SOKAR ACT` name hero is gone (district rule); his name appears only in the sponsor line, as the genre requires.
- Every non-Capitol fact is gone (honors, debate, Harvard, research, translation, the startup, Fresno community, photo, contact).
- The tracker no longer claims anything became law: its stages are his own steps, and the only "signature" is the visitor's.
- The "press run" intro is gone; the cartoon plays in place instead (and is skipped for reduced motion).
