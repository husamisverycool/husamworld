# Study, round 2: The Polling Station (`n1.html`)

Studied 2026-09-26. Firecrawl screenshots (1920×1080) saved in `world/_shots/n1-study/`
(distill.png, owid.png, iop.png, 538.png). Distill's own stylesheet read from GitHub
(`distillpub/template/src/styles/*.css`). The NYT page refuses scrapers ("we do not support this
site"), so the Upshot "You Draw It" mechanics come from the r2d3 / `youdrawitR` reimplementation

and 2017 pieces. 538's p-hacking piece is dead at its URL (redirects to ABC News); studied via the
Wayback Machine capture of 29 Dec 2019.

## 1. Distill.pub, "Why Momentum Really Works" (Goh, 2017) + the Distill template
- **Grid** (from `styles-layout.css`, verbatim numbers): at ≥1180px, `[screen-start] 1fr [page-start
  kicker-start] 60px [middle-start] 60px [text-start kicker-end] 60px ×8 [text-end gutter-start] 60px
  [middle-end] 60px [page-end gutter-end] 1fr`, column gap 32px. So body text is 8 columns
  (8×60 + 7×32 = 704px), figures can break out to `page` (12 cols, ≈ 984px) and side notes live in
  the 2-column `gutter` to the right. ≥1000px: 50px columns, 16px gap. ≥768px: 45px columns, 16px
  gap. Phone: 8 × 1fr with 8px margins.
- **Type**: `html{font-size:14px; line-height:1.6em}`, 16px at ≥768px. Body copy in the article is a
  Georgia-style serif at ~19px/1.6, `rgba(0,0,0,.8)`. The title is Georgia-ish, ~50–56px, weight 400,
  centred on the momentum piece, left on the template. Figure captions 12px (13px ≥1024px),
  `rgba(0,0,0,.6)`, sans. `.kicker/.marker` 15px 600 `rgba(0,0,0,.5)`. Links `#004276`.
- **Byline**: a hairline-ruled row under the hero figure with small uppercase labels
  (AUTHORS / AFFILIATIONS / PUBLISHED / DOI) at ~10px letterspaced, values at 13px, separated by
  1px vertical rules. On the momentum piece: "GABRIEL GOH / UC Davis | April. 4 2017 | Citation: Goh, 2017".
- **Hero figure**: an interactive figure *is* the lede. Contour plot in grey-blue steps
  (#e8edf2 → #9fb0c3), one orange path (#ff6600) with 3px dots, orange draggable handle with a
  hand icon and a white ring. Two sliders under it (orange thumb 12px, grey 2px track, tick labels
  at 0 / middle / max in 11px grey), and the standfirst paragraph sits *beside* the sliders in the
  third column. The reader changes a parameter and the drawing answers immediately. No "submit".
- **Citations** in square brackets [1, 2, 3] in link blue; footnotes as superscript numbers; hover
  pops a small card. **Appendix** at the end: grey background band, 2-column grid with small
  headings (Acknowledgments, Author Contributions, Citation, Updates and Corrections) in 15px.
- **What makes it memorable**: the text and the figure argue together. Every figure has a short
  bold caption lead ("Figure 3. ...") and an honest statement of what's plotted.
- **With Husam's content**: a working paper about his research record, where the figures are the
  argument and the appendix states exactly how everything was counted.

## 2. The Upshot, "You Draw It" (2015 college chances by income; 2017 Obama-era series)
- A chart shows the start of a series. The rest of the plot is covered by a **pale yellow band**
  (≈ #fff3c4) with a dashed leading edge, a small hand-drawn arrow and the words "Draw your
  guess" / "Draw the rest of the line". The reader drags left→right; the line (thick, ~3px, yellow-
  orange, dotted while drawing in the 2017 version) snaps to the x-grid. The yellow disappears
  under the drawn part; gaps stay yellow, so the reader always knows what's left to do.
- When the band is fully covered, a button appears: **"Show me how I did"** (NYT: grey rounded
  button). The true line animates in (≈ 1s, left to right) in a dark colour, and a sentence
  appears under the chart that is specific to *your* guess ("You were close" / "You guessed too
  low"). 2015 also showed a heat-map of everyone else's lines.
- Axes are Franklin Gothic (nyt-franklin) 12–13px grey; headline in Cheltenham; gridlines
  hairline grey; the y-axis labels sit above the lines, left-aligned, with the unit on the top
  tick only ("80%").
- **What makes it memorable**: you commit before you see. It turns reading into a test of your
  own assumption, and the reveal is personal.
- **Honesty rule for Husam**: only a series fully determined by CONTENT.md can be drawn. The
  one that qualifies: his research posts counted by the year they began (every post has a start
  year in the fact sheet). The reveal must also exist as a table so nothing is hidden.

## 3. FiveThirtyEight, "Hack Your Way To Scientific Glory" (Aschwanden & King, 2015)
- Background: a **light graph-paper grid** (≈ #f0f0f0 lines on #f7f7f7, 10px squares). Content
  column ~985px, left-aligned headline in a heavy grotesque (Atlas Grotesk Bold ~40px, #222).
- Steps are **numbered in filled black circles** (24px, white numeral) next to condensed
  uppercase monospace labels ("CHOOSE A POLITICAL PARTY", Decima Mono ~16px, tracked +.08em).
  Here the numbers are real order: you must do 1 before 4.
- Controls are **square checkboxes drawn as an ✕ in a box** (18px), 1px black rule between groups,
  small grey helper text under an option. Party buttons: flat red (#ff5a3c-ish) filled vs outlined.
- The scatter updates instantly; the verdict "Result: Almost" is a big bold word in grey, and a
  p-value meter with tick marks from 1.00 to 0.05 has a green zone at the end.
- Copy tone: second person, a little wry, then honest ("If you're interested in reading real
  (and more rigorous) studies..."). Sources listed in small grey type at the bottom.
- **With Husam's content**: the reader plays researcher; the numbered, ✕-box instrument is how a
  pre-registration form could look.

## 4. Pew Research Center topline questionnaire (PDF), and the Harvard Youth Poll pages
- Pew topline: title block "Topline questionnaire", survey name, field dates, **N=4,787** in
  caps. Every question has an **ID in caps** ("SHOP4."), a routing line above it in caps
  ("ASK ALL:", "ASK IF ONLINE SHOPPER (SHOP1a=1 ...):"), instructions in brackets
  ("[RANDOMIZE]"), then a two-column table: percentages right-aligned in a narrow column on the
  left, response labels on the right. "NET" rows, "Refused", "No answer", and "*" for less than
  half a percent. Deadpan, exact, no adjectives.
- IOP Youth Poll site (iop.harvard.edu): white page, serif display headings (their theme uses a
  Playfair-like face, which is banned here), crimson rule under each card heading (#CC0033-ish,
  4px), "Read more »" links in crimson, a left section nav with a pale-blue selected bar
  (#dff1f8) and a line drawing of the Capitol in pale blue behind it. Editions are numbered
  ("51st Edition, Fall 2025"), which is the one convention worth borrowing: numbered waves.
- **Honesty note**: the poll's own findings and sample sizes are *not* in CONTENT.md, so none of
  them can appear. Only "America's largest poll of 18–29-year-olds" and his role.

## 5. Paper ballots and Scantron sheets (from real objects)
- Scantron Form 882-E and the "red drop-out ink" family: ink in a single colour (green #3f8f5f
  or red-pink #d9475a) that the optical reader ignores, on off-white paper; **black timing marks**
  (≈ 3×10mm bars) down one edge, one per answer row, which the reader actually uses; bubbles are
  **ovals with the letter printed inside** (A B C D E), ~5×3.5mm, in rows of five; the row number
  sits left in bold. A "USE NO. 2 PENCIL ONLY" band, an example box showing RIGHT ● and WRONG ⊘ ✓ ✗
  marks, a name box with a grid of letter bubbles, and a "KEY" row. Everything is condensed
  grotesque (Helvetica Condensed / Univers Condensed), tiny caps.
- US paper ballots (ES&S / Hart): contest headers in **white on a black band**, "Vote for One" in
  italic under the office, ovals to the *left* of each choice, instructions box at the top-left
  with a drawing of a correctly filled oval, a perforated stub with a serial number.
- **What makes them memorable**: the physical act. Filling a bubble with a pencil mark (graphite
  grey, slightly irregular edge, a little outside the line) is universally recognisable.
- **With Husam's content**: a short answer sheet where every wrong option is still something
  from his record. The reader "feeds" it to the reader and gets it back graded.
