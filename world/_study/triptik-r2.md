## 1. AAA TripTik (the round-1 page, `world/triptik.html`, shots `world/_shots/triptik-desktop-a-*.png`)

- **Format:** a spiral-bound booklet of narrow strip maps, one strip per stretch of road, each strip redrawn north-up-ish so the route runs the long way down the page. A travel counselor marks the route with a fluorescent highlighter over a red road with a dark casing.
- **Round-1 specifics that worked:** Overpass (the closest Google match to Highway Gothic) at 900 weight for signs; green guide signs `#0b6b3e` with a 2px white inner keyline (`box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 3px green, inset 0 0 0 5px #fff`); paper `#f7f4e8` with a feTurbulence grain; highlighter `rgba(214,255,20,.62)` with `mix-blend-mode:multiply`; road `#dc3b2c` on casing `#7a160f`; Nanum Pen Script in pen blue `#1f3a9e` for the counselor's hand.
- **What made it memorable:** the highlighter wipe along the route and the handwriting in the margins. What did not: it was about the whole route, it had a car, it held other pages' facts.
- **With his content now:** a TripTik can only honestly draw home if the maps stay schematic. There is no "route" between his commitments he ever told us, and we must not invent streets for real organizations. A strip that runs the length of the valley (Fresno at one end, Clovis tucked northeast, Sierra to the east) is honest; a strip that turns left on a named street to the Food Bank is not.

**Take:** the highlighter wipe and the pen-blue margin notes. They are my devices and nobody else's.

## 2. Sanborn fire-insurance maps (Library of Congress; key sheet from guides.loc.gov/fire-insurance-maps/sanborn-interpreting)

- **Color code (from the key):** pink/red `~#f0a3b3` brick; yellow `~#f4e46c` frame (wood); blue `~#8fc1e3` stone and concrete; olive green `~#b9d3a0` "fire-resistive" and specials; gray `~#b8b3a8` adobe or iron. Colors are flat washes inside a thin black outline; the outline is heavier (2px) when a wall is fire-proof.
- **Type:** the KEY title is a big hand-cut condensed serif in a notched banner ("KEY" sits in a pennant cut into the frame). Captions are a small engraved roman, labels inside boxes are tiny sans caps ("(C.B.)", "(CONC.)", "D = DWELLING", "S = STORE"). Numbers of stories sit in the corner of each footprint; "D" and "S" are occupancy letters inside the footprint.
- **Layout:** double-rule frame (1px + 4px) around every panel; a "REFERENCE" panel of dense justified text; each sheet has a large sheet number in a corner box and a scale bar. A **key map** (index) shows the whole town cut into numbered sheets.
- **Tone:** clerical, exact, unsentimental. The map doesn't describe, it classifies.
- **With his content:** the *key* is the transferable idea, not the street plan. A legend that classifies his home life by kind of work (food, school, fundraising, campus) in Sanborn washes would read as a real key and as a joke only he could make. Drawing actual building footprints for the MCA or the Food Bank would be inventing locations, so no footprints.

**Take:** the KEY panel with flat washes and occupancy letters, the double-rule frame, and the numbered "sheet" convention.

## 3. Massimo Vignelli / Unimark, NYC subway diagram, 1972 (nytransitmuseum.org/vignelli; Wikipedia "New York City Subway map")

- **Geometry:** every line runs at 0, 45 or 90 degrees; stations at regular intervals, not geographic distance; "no dot, no stop" (Goldstein, 1965). Parallel services run as separate colored lines side by side, the same width, a fixed gap between them.
- **Stations:** a black dot on each line; interchanges are black dots joined by a black bar. Names in Helvetica Medium (Trade Gothic from 1974), set flush to one side, horizontal only.
- **Land and water:** land is a pale beige `~#efe6d3`; water a tan-brown `~#d9c5a0` (critics hated the brown rivers); boroughs named in larger type ("Manhattan", "Queens") floating in the land. Grid reference letters A–J down the side and 1–10 along the bottom, in a thin frame.
- **The pocket guide:** a key strip across the top ("How to Use This Map", "Key", then service columns per division), and a verso with a *service breakdown* per route: stops, times, notes. Route "bullets": colored circles with a white letter or number.
- **Quote worth keeping in mind:** "You want to go from Point A to Point B, period. The only thing you are interested in is the spaghetti." (Vignelli, 2006.)
- **Why it failed for riders:** people cross-checked it against the real street grid and felt lost. That is exactly the risk for Fresno: a diagram that looks like geography invites people to read it as geography.
- **With his content:** five lines (Food Bank, Saturday School, MCA, MSA, tutoring) with bullets. The brief's "weekdays are stations" only has facts for two lines (Sundays, Saturdays); I do not know which days MCA, MSA or tutoring ran, so a weekday station for them would be invented. Also a row of weekday stations is uncomfortably close to the Plaza's week grid. Years as stations are honest for every line.

**Take:** route bullets, 45/90 discipline, "no dot, no stop", and the service-breakdown verso as a way to hold the numbers.

## 4. Central Valley fruit-crate and grape-lug labels (Calisphere / LAPL Fruit Crate Label Collection; PBS SoCal "15 Fascinating Fruit Crate Labels")

- **Formats:** citrus box end ~10×11 in (near square); **grape lug labels are a long strip ~3.3:1** (Valley Queen, Talk O' the Town, Fat Pak; growers in Exeter and the southern valley). The long strip format is itself a strip map's cousin.
- **Color:** deep ultramarine/navy ground `~#1c2a6e`, cream-yellow letters `~#f6e7a1`, tomato red `~#d8412f`, grape purple `~#6c2a57`, leaf green `~#3d6b3a`, sun orange `~#f08a24`. A thin cream keyline 6–8px inside the edge; sometimes an olive outer border.
- **Type:** fat block capitals with a 3D extrusion (red or black, offset down-right, 4–8 layers), often **arched** or set in perspective ("Talk O' the Town"); a flowing script brand name with a black outline ("Valley Queen"); tiny caps "BRAND" tucked under or beside the name; a bottom band in small condensed caps: "GROWER & PACKER · EXETER, CALIFORNIA", "PRODUCE OF U.S.A.".
- **Imagery:** a sun low on the horizon with rays, the Sierra in violet-blue behind rows of orchards or vines converging in perspective, a vignette circle holding a portrait or a bunch of grapes.
- **Tone:** boastful, local, proud of place. The label names the town it came from in the bottom band, always.
- **With his content:** this is the valley's own graphic language, and it lets the page brag in a way that feels local instead of corporate: "FRESNO · CLOVIS" on a lug label, the MCA's numbers set like a brand's claims. No grower names are invented; the bottom band says only true things ("Fresno & Clovis, California", "Central Valley").

**Take:** the long lug-label strip as the header format, the extruded arched caps, the bottom band that names the place.

## 5. Community bulletin boards and flyers (mosque lobbies, campus boards, library corkboards; from life, not a single site)

- **Materials:** cork `~#c49a6c` with darker flecks; flyers on white, canary `#fff3a0`, pink `#ffc7d1`, goldenrod and green copier paper; pushpins (red, yellow, blue, clear) casting a small hard shadow; staples; strips of tape; a layer of older flyers underneath with torn corners.
- **Mechanics:** tear-off tabs along the bottom edge (vertical text on each tab, cut lines between them), a fundraising thermometer poster colored in by hand with marker as money comes in, a sign-up sheet on a clipboard, a hand-lettered "THANK YOU" card.
- **Type:** photocopied sans headlines (Franklin Gothic, Helvetica Condensed), marker hand lettering, an ALL CAPS date line.
- **With his content:** the MCA is exactly the organization whose life happens on a bulletin board: events, clothing and food drives, a fundraising thermometer for refugees and flood victims, a pledge for a transitional home. The thermometer is honest for "$240K+ raised" only if it has no invented goal; I would draw it filled to the top with the number written above, as a "we did it" poster.

**Take:** tear-off tabs that actually tear, and the marker-filled thermometer used as a celebration, not a chart.

## 6. Maggie Appleton's hand-drawn explainers (maggieappleton.com; "A Brief History & Ethos of the Digital Garden")

- **Branding scrape:** background `#F6F5F1`, text `#4A4A46`, accent/link plum `#5F023E`, logo teal `#04A4BA`; headings Canela Deck (h1 81.8px), body Lato 15px (essays set larger, ~20px serif); 2px radii; generous 6px base unit.
- **Illustration style:** soft textured fills, teal + olive-yellow + plum on off-white, objects drawn slightly isometric, **hand-drawn arrows** that curve between parts, loose ink outlines that don't close perfectly. Labels are handwritten, next to what they label, with a small arrow.
- **Structure:** a sticky table of contents on the left, a single wide illustration up top, then the essay; margin notes ("6ya") in teal inline.
- **With his content:** the annotation voice. Handwritten labels with drawn arrows pointing at the things on the map ("most Sundays", "every Saturday I could"). Not her colors or fonts, just the idea that the drawing explains itself with arrows and hand labels.

**Take:** hand-drawn arrows plus handwritten labels that point at things, drawn with a slightly wobbly SVG line.
