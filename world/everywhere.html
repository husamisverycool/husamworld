Signs:

Every program has a sign, drawn on a square grid with lines at 0°, 45° and 90°, the way Otl Aicher drew the Munich and Frankfurt pictograms in 1972. The colour says what kind of thing it is.

  • Blue: air traffic. The programs that reach abroad.

  • Green: services. The public-service and campus fellowships.

  • White: everything else. The institutions he works inside.

Set in Fira Code Light; the boards in Cabin, standing in for the Gill Sans Bold Solari silk-screened on its flaps. The globe and the code are computed one character cell at a time, after Andreas Gysin's ASCII playground (play.ertdfgcvb.xyz); the code changes letter by letter, the way his home page changes its words. The boards turn whole-word flaps, as the Solari boards did. The signs follow Otl Aicher's grid and his Frankfurt Airport colours. The land comes from Natural Earth; the shading follows the sun right now. The three-letter codes are country codes, not airports.
